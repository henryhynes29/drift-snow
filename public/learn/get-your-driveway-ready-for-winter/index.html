Get your driveway ready for winter

Ten minutes in the fall saves a cracked curb, a torn-up lawn or a buried well cap in January.

Mark the edges

Once the snow is down, nobody can see where the driveway ends and the lawn begins. Driveway stakes or reflective markers along the edges (and at any sharp turns) keep the blade on the pavement.

Flag anything a plow could hit

  • Curbs and raised or broken concrete
  • Well caps, septic and valve covers
  • Landscape edging, rocks and lighting
  • Sprinkler heads and anything left outside

On DRIFT you mark these once on your property map with pins, lines and areas, and every driver sees them before they accept.

Decide where the snow goes

Pick a spot on your own property with room for a whole winter of piles, away from where melt will run back across the driveway and refreeze. In Duluth, snow can't go into the street or alley.

Clear the way before a storm

  • Move cars to one side of the driveway or off it if you can.
  • Bring in trash cans, hoses and decorations.
  • Don't forget the sidewalk: Duluth gives you 24 hours after the snow stops. See the rules.
