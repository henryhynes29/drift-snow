How to price snow plowing jobs

Guessing loses money on big driveways and loses customers on small ones. Here's how to price by the work.

Start with the area

The plowed area drives the time on site. DRIFT measures it from the customer's outline on a satellite map and recommends a price from a base plus a per-square-foot rate. Past a point, the rate per square foot drops by half so big driveways don't get priced out.

DrivewayTypical recommended total per storm
1-car driveway$49–$54
2-car driveway$58–$64
Long driveway$65–$72
Huge driveway$76–$85

Totals include the $10 call-out fee (which goes to the driver) and DRIFT's $5 booking fee.

Add for slope

Steep driveways take longer and need more care on the way down. In the app, a moderate or steep slope raises the recommended price.

Add for snow depth

Deep snow is heavier and slower. A simple rule: about +15% for 4–8 inches, +30% for 8–12 inches and +50% for a foot or more.

Count the drive

A $50 job 15 minutes away can pay worse per hour than a $40 job next door. Group jobs by area, and on DRIFT set a home zone so you mostly see jobs near you.

Have a minimum

Every stop costs you time to get there, set up and leave. A call-out fee or minimum covers it. On DRIFT, every job includes a $10 call-out that goes entirely to the driver.
