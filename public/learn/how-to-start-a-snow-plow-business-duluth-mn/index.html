How to start a snow plow business in Duluth, MN

Got a truck that can take a plow? Here's how to turn it into a real side business in Duluth: the gear, the paperwork, insurance, taxes and where the customers come from.

1. The truck and the plow

Most people start with a half-ton or three-quarter-ton pickup and a straight or V-plow sized for that truck. Before you buy, check your truck's owner's manual or the plow maker's fit guide: plows are rated for specific trucks, and an overloaded front end is hard on steering and suspension.

Duluth adds two things to think about: hills and tight spaces. Good winter tires, ballast in the bed, and a plow you can angle into narrow driveways matter more here than raw blade width.

2. Set up the business

You can plow as a sole proprietor under your own name, but many people form an LLC to keep business and personal money separate. In Minnesota an LLC costs $155 to file online or in person, or $135 by mail, and the annual renewal is free but has to be filed every year or the LLC is dissolved.

An EIN (employer identification number) from the IRS is free and lets you open a business bank account without using your Social Security number on everything.

The City of Duluth's business licensing page lists specific licensed trades (tree service, wrecker services and others) and doesn't list snow plowing. Call the City Clerk at (218) 730-5500 to confirm for your situation.

3. Get the right insurance

This is the step people skip and regret. Many personal auto policies exclude plowing and other business use, which can leave you personally on the hook for an accident or damage. Talk to an agent about commercial auto and general liability coverage. More on plow insurance.

4. Price your jobs

Price by the work, not a flat guess: area, slope, snow depth and drive time all matter. Here's how to price snow plowing jobs. On DRIFT, customers see a recommended price from their mapped driveway and you see the pay before you accept.

5. Find customers

The hard part of a plow business isn't plowing, it's filling the route. You can knock doors, post in neighborhood groups and sign seasonal contracts, or you can pick up on-demand jobs. With DRIFT you go online when it snows, see open jobs near you with the pay, distance and a map of the driveway, and take the ones that fit. Customers can save you as a favorite, so good work turns into regulars.

6. Taxes

Plowing income is taxable income. Snow plowing isn't subject to sales tax in Minnesota or Wisconsin, but you'll owe income tax and self-employment tax on your profit, and you can deduct business miles. Read the tax guide.

7. Safety and good habits

  • Walk new properties in daylight before the first storm if you can, or study the customer's map and hazard pins.
  • Keep the blade up a little on gravel.
  • Never push snow into the street. Duluth doesn't allow it and Minnesota law prohibits snow on public roads.
  • Take before and after photos on every job.

Not legal or tax advice. Rules change. Check with the Minnesota Secretary of State, the City of Duluth, an insurance agent and a tax pro for your situation.

Questions people ask

Do I need a license to plow snow in Duluth?

The City of Duluth's business licensing page doesn't list snow plowing among its licensed businesses. Confirm with the City Clerk at (218) 730-5500.

How much does it cost to form an LLC in Minnesota?

$155 online or in person, or $135 by mail. The annual renewal is free but required.

Do I charge sales tax on snow plowing in Minnesota?

Minnesota lists snow plowing as a non-taxable service. Check with the Department of Revenue or a tax pro if you sell other services too.
